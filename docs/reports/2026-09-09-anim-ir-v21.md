# anim-ir v21: a still figure read back — what a vision reader recovers from the picture (2026-09-09)

## Question

Every still on the corpus is green three ways: `check` (well-formed), `layout`
(nothing drawn on anything), `check --expect` (the picture matches its facts as
compiled). None of the three asks whether a **reader** gets the facts back from
the pixels. v12 put vision readers on animation contact sheets and asked "what
is broken"; this round puts them on still figures and asks "what does it say":
every box, every container and what is directly inside it, which container is
inside which, every arrow tail → head, what is red and dashed, what is lit.
The reading is scored against the facts the scene draws, name by name, and
the misreadings are the round's findings.

## What was added

- **`review --still`** (the default for a `modules` / `diagram` scene with no
  walk): the figure as `<name>.still.png`, a brief that asks for it read back
  as JSON, and `<name>.facts.json` — `sceneFacts`, what the scene draws in the
  shape `check --expect` reads, plus each id's label and each group's parent,
  because a reader sees labels and boxes inside boxes, not ids and `parent`
  fields. `--answers a.json` (or `--model M`) scores the reading into
  `<name>.still-score.md`: per field, read / missed / invented; a dependency
  read the other way round; a module put in the wrong container; a forbidden
  arrow read as a plain one; a name that resolves to nothing drawn (labels
  resolve to ids exactly, or as one label's whole word — `gateway` for
  `API gateway`); and one number, **fidelity** = read ÷ (facts + invented).
- **`layout` reports `boxes`**: two outlined containers that cross where
  neither holds the other. Containers were never checked — texts were.

## Material

Nine still figures: seven writers' module maps from v13–v20 (`fa`, `fe`,
`ga`, `hb`, `hd`, `ld`, `nc`), the nested-groups fixture, and the workspace
map `vlmkit-anim repo` draws (`arch`, a `diagram`). Every reader was a fresh
subagent given one PNG and one brief and nothing else, as every round's
writers have been; the smaller model read three figures, the larger all nine.

## What the readers found

Four things the geometry could not see, each in a figure `layout` called clean:

1. **Two containers crossing** (`fe`): "the 'Core domain' container and the
   'Adapters (driven side)' container do not nest cleanly — their borders
   overlap, and 'Repository port (interface)' sits inside both borders at
   once." The writer had drawn the forbidden edge as a plain `line`, so
   `postgres` landed one layer below `domain`; the adapters then owned that
   layer alone and were drawn as a full-width row — straight through the
   column the core group spans. The port is inside one container; the picture
   says two.
2. **A container label at a bottom corner reads as something else**: "the
   container label 'core' sits at the bottom-left of its own box, under the
   'domain model' box … at a glance it reads like a caption for the box above
   it" (nested fixture); `fe`'s "'Core domain' label is written below the
   Repository port (interface) box … reads as floating text under that box";
   and the smaller model read `nc`'s low `kernel` label as a module, gave it
   three arrows, and put the kernel's two modules in `services`.
3. **A forbidden arrow filed as a highlight** (`hb`, `fe`): the brief asked for
   "anything in a strong accent colour" and the red dashed arrow is exactly
   that. The reader saw the claim and had no field for it.
4. **A fan of arrows out of one corner** — the top difficulty on every dense
   map: "six-plus lines fan out of 'vlmkit (root CLI)' and bunch tightly
   together as they cross the middle of the image, so pairing each line to its
   exact head (mcp vs. generate vs. plan vs. ai) is a best-effort read rather
   than a certain one" (`fa`); "all lines leave the same corner of the box at
   slightly different angles" (`ld`); "two arrowheads land almost on top of
   each other at animation-eval's bottom edge" (`arch`).

The dense maps' scores are the arrows they lost: `fa` missed nine of 26 and
invented four; `hd` missed 14 and invented 13, all of them among the arrows
into `ai` and `core`; the modules, groups and nesting on every figure were
read right by the larger model.

## What changed from it

- **The band layout bands both groups** when one would otherwise be a
  full-width row across layers another group spans, and **`layout` reports
  `boxes`** when two containers cross anyway. `fe` now draws the core column
  and the adapters column side by side.
- **A container label stays in a top corner**: when both top corners are
  taken, the container grows sideways, up to 48px, until one is clear —
  inside its parent, off every other container. The nested fixture's `core`
  label is at its top-left again.
- **The brief asks for `forbidden`** (red dashed arrows, tail → head) and the
  scorer reads it: a forbidden arrow listed as a plain dependency is "read as
  plain", one listed as forbidden is read. After the change all three readers
  on figures with a forbidden arrow filed it correctly.
- **Several edges at one box spread along its side**, tails and heads, ordered
  by where the far end lies so the fan does not cross itself. A `relate` arc
  that then ran through a container label at its own end (the web-service
  fixture's "infrastructure", once that label had moved to the top-left) is
  handled by the annotator **moving the label** to another corner of its
  container — an arc cannot bulge away from a text at its endpoint.

The corpus layout check is unchanged by all of it: every scene as clean as
before, and the new `boxes` rule fires on none.

## What the second reading says

- **Fidelity 1.0 stays 1.0** on the three figures that had it, and every
  forbidden arrow is now read as one.
- **The dense maps moved little**: `fa` 0.75 → 0.83, `arch` 0.64 → 0.68, `ld`
  0.77 → 0.76, `hd` 0.56 → 0.51 — inside the noise of a best-effort read. The
  readers say why in the same words as before: "individual line identity is
  genuinely hard to verify where 6+ lines overlap near the 'ai' and 'core'
  boxes" (`hd`). Spreading the tails along a box's bottom does not unbundle
  seven arrowheads landing on a 40px box. And `ld`'s nine-arrow fan out of
  `index` in an `lr` layout leaves a **36px side**: there is no room to spread,
  so the compiler left it ("the 9 arrows … all leave from almost the same
  point on its right edge").
- **`fe` lost one arrow** (0.83 → 0.75): the long `app-svc → repo-port` edge,
  now leaving the box's left side at mid-height rather than its bottom-left
  corner, was read as coming from the `HTTP handler` arrow that lands at that
  corner. One reading; the side is the geometric one the edge already left
  from, so this is noted, not reverted.
- **The smaller model is still not a reader** (v12's finding): 0.35 on `hd`,
  and on the fixed nested fixture it reports two arrows "through the 'core'
  label" that the geometry, and the larger model, do not see.

So: reading fidelity is a **density** measurement first. Up to ~16 arrows a
figure reads back at 0.76–1.0; at 26 arrows on 11 boxes it reads at 0.5–0.8
whatever the routing, and the readers name the bundle every time. The fix for
that is not in edge geometry: it is a different picture — an adjacency matrix,
edge bundling, or a map split by group — and that waits for a brief that asks.
