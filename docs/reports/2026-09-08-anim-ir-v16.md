# anim-ir v16: a still figure's own vocabulary — tone, implements, equals (2026-09-08)

## Question

Two asks from the v13 still-figure writers were left open through v14 and v15:

> gb: "No colour field on `deps` edges — coloring one edge requires reaching
> into the beat/sequence/highlight machinery on what's supposed to be a
> motion-free still figure."

> ga: "`modules` has vocabulary for 'depends on' and 'belongs in this
> container,' but nothing for 'satisfies the same interface as its sibling' …
> there's no op that states 'these two are equivalent/substitutable' the way
> `relate` states 'these two are connected.'"

Both are about a picture that does not move. A `highlight` step is a beat; a
still has none, and a writer who wants one arrow to stand out should not have
to invent a sequence for it. This round adds the vocabulary and asks two fresh
writers to draw the figure that needs all of it.

## What was added

- **`tone`** on modules, diagram nodes, dependencies and edges: `accent`
  (a module is filled with the highlight colour; an arrow and its label take
  it), `bad` (the `bad` colour), `muted` (greyed outline and label — a test
  double, a deprecated module). A colour role, not a colour: the theme
  decides the pixels. A thing with `"tone": "accent"` counts as lit for `check
  --expect`, so a fact sheet's `highlighted` list covers stills too.
- **`"style": "implements"`** on a dependency or edge: dashed with a hollow
  head — the UML realisation — laid out like a real dependency. The SVG
  renderer and the runtime gained the outlined marker (`head: "hollow"` on a
  timeline node).
- **`"style": "equals"`** on `relate`: a double line, no head, between two
  anchors that are equivalent / substitutable / satisfy the same interface.

Fixture `modules-ports-adapters.json` shows all three at once: the domain
filled, the in-memory adapter muted, `app → port` in the accent colour with
its label, both adapters realising the port, the forbidden `domain → postgres`
in red, and `=` between the adapters labelled "substitutable".

## The writers

One brief, `modules-adapters-still`, written to need every new word: five
things a reader must see in the figure itself — the adapters *implement* the
port in UML notation; the in-memory adapter is a test double and visually
secondary; the two adapters are substitutable, said between them as a
relation; `app → port` is the rule the eye lands on; `domain → postgres` is
forbidden — with a fact sheet that fixes what may be lit. Two fresh writers.

> jb: "All five were guide-confirmed, none guessed. … Nothing in the brief

The two v13 asks are closed as asked: gb's one coloured edge on a motion-free
figure is `"tone": "accent"` on the dependency, and ga's "these two are
equivalent" is `relate` with `"style": "equals"`.

### A caveat the writer raised about the round itself

> jb: "the guide's worked examples for `modules` use the same module names
> (`domain`, `postgres`) as this brief's forbidden edge, and the
> `relate`/`equals`/'substitutable' example uses `postgres`/`memory` too, so
> the brief maps onto the doc's own illustrations almost one-to-one."

True, and it weakens what a first-round green proves here: the guide's new
paragraph was written against the same architecture the brief describes, so a
writer could copy rather than compose. The v13 ports-and-adapters brief had
the same shape before this paragraph existed and cost two writers a round
each, so the vocabulary is what changed; but a fair measure of *discovery*
needs a brief on a different architecture, and the next round that touches
stills should use one.

### Friction

> jb: "the guide doesn't spell out whether `tone: accent` on a `deps`
> long-form entry needs `"style": "arrow"` stated explicitly or whether
> omitting `style` (default `arrow`) is fine when only `tone` is set."

The `deps` row now says so. Nothing else was reported; ja's list of what did
not help reads "Nothing."

### What the round did not settle

- The brief mirrors the guide's example (above). Discovery is unmeasured.
- `tone` is a role with three values. A writer who wants "the module we are
  deleting" and "the module we are adding" in one figure has one `bad` and
  one `accent` and nothing for a third distinction; whether that is asked for
  is unknown.
- `equals` is a straight double line or a doubled arc; when the relation has
  to bend around a bystander the two strokes are offset copies of one curve,
  which is right for a shallow arc and visibly wrong for a tight one. No
  fixture exercises the tight case.
- The `expect` semantics: an accent-toned module counts as lit. The fixture
  `modules-ports-adapters`, which fills the domain, fails the adapters brief's
  sheet for exactly that reason — the hint now names `tone` as a cause — and
  that is the check doing its job, but a sheet author has to know that
  `highlighted` and `tone: accent` are one thing.
