# anim-ir v18: fact sheets for the walked kinds, one written from the code — and what four writers found in the compilers (2026-09-08)

## Question

`check --expect` (v14) read module maps and diagrams: modules, dependencies,
the forbidden one, what is lit, group members. The walked kinds had nothing —
a Dijkstra that visited nodes in the wrong order, a trace that ended in the
wrong state, a protocol that delivered a message the brief said was lost were
all green. And the only fact sheet the tool could write itself came from
`package.json` files; inside a package the truth is the import statements.

This round gives `graph`, `state-machine` and `distributed` scenes sheets of
their own, adds `vlmkit-anim facts <dir>` (a sheet from a directory's import
graph), and asks four writers to draw against them.

## What was added

- **Sheets for the walked kinds.** A graph sheet has `nodes`, `edges` (`"a->b"`;
  either way on an undirected graph, `"a<->b"` says so), `visited` (the order
  of visits), `path` (the nodes lit at the end), `labels` and `highlighted`. A
  state-machine sheet has `states`, `transitions` (`"a->b:event"`), `initial`,
  `final`, `visited` and `end`. A distributed sheet has `nodes`, `messages` in
  order (`"a->b:label"`), `lost` and each node's final `status`. Each fact is
  read from where its truth is — the scene for what is declared, the compiled
  walk (`meta.visited`, `meta.path`) for what it did, the final frame for what
  the reader sees — and a sheet field the kind does not have is one error, not
  silence. Ordered facts report the first position that differs, once.
- **`vlmkit-anim facts <dir> [--depth 1] [--tests] [--out f.json]`**: the
  entries at that depth are the modules (a directory is one, a file is one),
  every relative import that crosses between two of them is a dependency;
  `*.test.*` and `__tests__/` are skipped unless asked for. On this
  repository: `packages/vlmkit-capture/src` is 15 modules and 16 dependencies
  from 15 files; `packages/vlmkit-anim/src` 19 and 61; `src/cli` 6 and 7.

**The sheets did their job and were never the problem.** la's facts matched on
the first run — `algorithm: "dijkstra"` with `start` and `goal` produced the
visit order and the path, no op was written by hand — and la called the
`--expect` line "genuinely excellent". lb's nine-state trace matched first
time. lc's ten messages, the lost one and the three statuses matched the
moment the scene compiled. ld's first run was 13 ✗: twelve `*.test.ts`
modules the writer had drawn that the sheet does not have, each named with the
fifteen valid ids, and the one line the sheet always adds when ids disagree.
ld: "actionable and decisive".

**Every round after the first was spent on the compilers**, and every one of
those was a defect this round fixed:

- **lb**: `⚠ "idle" is covered by token (38%)` at step 1. The token rested
  at the state's centre, on top of the label; a wide label ("CLOSED") hid the
  38% under the threshold, a short one did not. lb tried `tb` and `circle`
  (worse: 10 and 110 overlaps on a four-state machine), then typed a canvas
  size and four positions, which cannot move a token that follows its state.
  Fixed three ways: the token rests on the top of the state's rim; a circle's
  ring is sized so neighbours do not touch and the canvas grows with it (the
  ring had been shrunk to the frame, putting four states within 35px); a
  straight transition's label that would sit on a state — a bystander or its
  own end — takes a spot no state and no earlier label holds. lb's machine is
  now clean in all three layouts with no coordinate.
- **la**: the distance label sat below every node, and on the road map an
  edge ran through one in 17 of 18 frames. la tried the four layouts, then
  hand-permuted `nodes`, then brute-forced all 720 orders and took the
  fifteenth. The label now picks the side of its node no edge leaves from and
  no other node sits on; la's scene is clean in the brief's own node order.
  la also caught the `crossed` hint speaking `modules` — "that layer", "one
  group" — on a graph; it now speaks each kind's vocabulary.
- **lc**: `after: "ack"` — `"ack" labels 2 messages, so it cannot anchor
  anything`. The guide's timeout recipe assumed a unique label, and in a
  two-participant protocol every label is sent twice by design; lc guessed
  `after: "p2->coord:ack"`, which did not exist, and settled for an unanchored
  `note` as a timeout barrier. The guess is now the syntax: `from->to:label`,
  or `from->to` when the pair sends once; the error names the two choices.
- **ld**: the canvas-size warning said "on a 1280px-wide screen" while its
  percentage came from the larger side (proved by swapping `lr` and `tb`:
  width and height traded places, 44% both times), and offered `circle` to a
  `modules` scene that has no circle. It now names the side that is over,
  the pixel target, and the layouts the kind has.

Across the corpus, the compiler fixes cleared five scenes' remaining issues —
three v9/v10 state machines (cd, dc, dd: the token on a short label), lb, and
u's graph (a label under an edge) — and changed nothing else.

### What changed from it

- The four compiler fixes above and the two message fixes (crossed-edge hint
  per kind; canvas warning with side, pixel target and the kind's layouts).
- `after` accepts `from->to:label` / `from->to`; the guide's timeout paragraph
  and the `messages` row say so; the ambiguous-label error names the choices.
- The `facts` summary line says which files it skipped and the flag that
  includes them; the brief says test files are not part of the map.
- The stills paragraph names a `text` block without `at` as the way to say
  what a map leaves out — the one-beat `sequence` ld hesitated over is the
  intended form, and `still` renders the last frame.

### What the round did not settle

- **Order sensitivity.** la is right that `circle` follows the `nodes` list
  and nothing says so beyond the new hint; with labels placing themselves the
  order matters less, but a graph dense enough for edges to cross labels on
  every side would still need it. la's ask — the tool searching orders
  itself, since `layout --json` has the count — is not built.
- **A message `id`.** lc wanted an identity separate from the label. The
  qualified anchor covers every duplicate a lane pair can produce except the
  same label sent twice between the same pair, which `from->to:label` cannot
  tell apart; that case waits for a scene that has it.
- **`facts` at depth 1 flattens.** A directory is one module, so a large
  package reads as a dozen boxes with `index` depending on all of them;
  `--depth 2` splits files out but multiplies edges. Which depth makes a
  readable map of a given tree is the writer's call and unmeasured.
- The sheets read the compiled walk, not the frames: a `visit` op that the
  renderer drew wrongly would still match. `layout` and the eval keep the
  frame side.
