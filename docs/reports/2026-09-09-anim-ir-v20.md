# anim-ir v20: a sequence diagram, and boxes inside boxes (2026-09-09)

## Question

The last two shapes on the user's list of what a diagramming tool still lacked:
a **sequence diagram** — participants across the top, messages in order down
the page, activation bars, `loop` / `alt` frames — and **nested containers**,
a group drawn inside another on a module map. v19's flowchart and gantt
writers had been green on the first write with the guide alone; this round
asks whether two kinds whose meaning lives in *nesting* (frames in frames,
groups in groups) read as well from one section each.

## What was added

- **`kind: sequence`.** `participants` (`actor` pills, `system` boxes) and
  `messages` in order: `call` (solid, filled head; activates the receiver until
  it returns), `return` (dashed, open head), `async` (solid, open head), a hook
  to oneself; `{"loop", "items"}` and `{"alt": [{"when", "items"}, …]}` draw a
  frame round their items, recursively; notes and every annotation op. Nothing
  is timed — order is the meaning — and each message is one beat captioned
  `shop → payment: charge(card)` (a return `shop ← payment: declined`). An
  activation bar starts where the call lands and grows row by row until that
  participant's return; each `alt` branch starts from the activations as they
  were when the frame opened, so a return in the second branch closes the same
  bar the first branch did, and the bar runs on. The check warns about a return
  nothing activated, a participant still activated at the end, one never
  messaged, an `alt` with one branch; `check --expect` reads `nodes` and
  `messages` in order, frames flattened.
- **Nested groups** on `modules` and `diagram`: `"parent"` on a group. The
  layout bands by the outermost group and keeps an inner group's members
  contiguous within a band; a parent's box wraps its children's with room for
  their labels (24px more padding per level below it); a module belongs to the
  innermost group. The validator names a missing parent, a self-parent and a
  circle. The `modules` canvas grows per nesting level — the first shop fixture
  had `backend`'s top running through `clients`.
- **`layout` sees a bar through a word.** The first sequence sheet drew
  `alt [c hed]`: the web lifeline's activation bar, 10px wide, down the
  middle of the frame's tag. By area that is 12% of the text, under the
  overlap threshold, so `layout` said clean. A narrow filled box that spans
  a text's height and lies strictly inside its width now counts as an
  overlap whatever the ratio; the sequence compiler pads a frame by its
  widest tag so its labels sit left of the first lifeline.

### Friction, verbatim

The round's friction is all documentation — three writers, no compiler
defect in their hands (the two the round did fix, above, were found on the
fixtures before the writers ran).

> na: "The `expect` sheet fields for `sequence` are not in the guide's own
> 'Checking a figure against the facts' walk-through table … the guide's prose
> right there ('Other kinds … have their own semantic checks and no sheet yet')
> reads as if `sequence` might be one of the 'no sheet yet' kinds."

> na: "Activation-bar close semantics aren't stated anywhere in the guide …
> the bar's height simply **stops growing** at the closing return — there's no
> shrink-to-zero animation, so 'closed' is a freeze, not a visual event, and a
> bar that closes on the very last message of the scene is indistinguishable at
> a glance from a bar that 'never closes.'"

> na: "the guide never explicitly says frames can nest inside frames — it
> would be worth one line confirming that `loop`/`alt` items are the same
> grammar as `messages`, recursively."

> nb: "The documentation says 'A `kind` on a call activates the receiver' but
> doesn't explicitly state *when* the activation bar appears or how long it
> extends."

> nb: "The guide doesn't specify what loop labels should contain or whether
> they're arbitrary text."

> nc: "`pnpm exec vlmkit-anim schema --kind modules` … omits the
> `groups[].parent` field entirely from its quick-reference `groups` line, even
> though the full guide documents it and the task depends on it; an agent
> relying on the terser schema output alone would have had no way to discover
> container nesting exists."

> nc: "the guide's 'Checking a figure against the facts' table doesn't state
> whether a fact sheet's `groups` field is meant to check nesting or just
> direct membership."

### What changed from it

- The sequence section says where a bar starts, that it grows a row per beat
  and **stops growing** at the return rather than vanishing, that `check` and
  not the picture says who is still activated, that frame labels are free
  text, and that `items` take the same entries as `messages`, recursively.
- The fact-sheet table has `sequence` and `flowchart` rows (the latter had
  landed in v19 without one).
- `schema --kind modules` and `--kind diagram` show `"parent"` on a group.
- The sheet's `groups` row says the members are each container's **own**, that
  nesting is not a fact the sheet checks, and where to confirm it.

### What the round did not settle

- **A bar that ends is a freeze.** na's point stands after the sentence: the
  picture cannot show "closed at the last message" against "still open". A
  closing tick or a rounded end on the bar's last row would; no writer asked
  for one yet, and the check names the still-open participants.
- **The sheet does not read nesting.** `groups` are own members. A
  `"parent": {"services": "platform"}` field would be one line in `expect.ts`
  and one row in the table; it waits for a brief where a writer nests the
  wrong way and the sheet stays green.
- **Frames in the sample are one deep.** The guide's example has an `alt`;
  the brief's `loop` holding an `alt` is covered by the sentence, not by an
  example. Two writers inferred the recursion correctly; whether a third
  would is the next round's data if the example stays as it is.
- With this round every kind on the list is drawn: sequence, flowchart,
  gantt, nested boxes. The next items in the user's order are vision readers
  on stills, a diff figure between two scenes, embedding (remark / VitePress,
  a mermaid import), and `why` — the compiler explaining a layout decision.
